# Senda 4.0 research and business-model audit

**Audit date:** 23 November 2026  
**Evidence cutoff in the research pack:** mostly 18 November 2026  
**Scope:** All research and product files in `docs/senda-4.0/research/` and `docs/senda-4.0/product/` (including `product/scripts/pilot-loop.sh`), plus `pivot.md`, `context/project-context.md`, `work-plan.md`, and the testing pack. Implementation statements were cross-checked against `apps/web/README.md`, Prisma schema, Connect/auth/proof code, relevant API routes, and seed data. No application code or Git state was changed.

## Executive assessment

The best-supported strategic choice is **UK → Zimbabwe private-school fees as a discovery wedge**, not as a quantified market or proven customer segment. Official corridor data establishes material remittance flow into Zimbabwe, and a 2022 UK-diaspora survey supports education as a stated purpose. Neither measures UK→Zimbabwe school-fee dollars, the number of fee-paying diaspora households, or demand for Senda.

The proposed job—help a payer and school associate a payment with the right student and bill—has credible face validity. The supply case is plausible from published school payment methods and local-school software workflows. However, the assertions that MTOs will buy an independent obligation/reference/proof layer, and that schools will operate a second ledger and complete KYB for it, remain **hypotheses**. No partner or bursar interviews, customer commitments, pilot schools, payment data, willingness-to-pay results, or live settlement evidence are documented.

The primary commercial risk is an evidence gap, not an architecture gap. Public competitors include direct-to-school/purpose-pay services and vertically integrated wallet offerings. They may solve more of the end-to-end job than Senda’s thinner product. Further, current “confirmed/proven” product behavior is a matched **partner attestation**, not independent evidence that a school received funds. The report should therefore sell “reconciled partner confirmation and an auditable payment record,” not guaranteed receipt or settlement certainty.

The stack is proportionate for a prototype and the relational workflow, but lock #9 is a founder preference supported by an internally scored fit assessment—not demonstrated market demand or a production readiness finding. #10’s bank/EcoCash destinations are sensible candidates, but payout to school-owned business accounts, custom-reference behavior, limits, and partner eligibility are unverified by named MTOs. Lock status is not the same as validation.

**Recommendation:** retain the school-fee wedge for a bounded discovery/pilot test, preserve the no-funds-handling doctrine pending written regulatory advice, and make live-pilot spend conditional on a named MTO, named schools, verified business payout paths, receipt-evidence design, and a tested commercial offer. Do not use the 15–20% diversion figure, broad diaspora estimates, competitor marketing, or simulated sandbox proofs as validated market or outcome metrics.

## Evidence standard and terminology

- **Primary evidence** means an official dataset/regulator document, original research, or a company’s own product/pricing documentation. Company marketing is primary evidence of what that company claims to offer, **not** of market adoption or performance.
- **Secondary evidence** means reporting, commentary, industry summaries, or Senda’s synthesis of other sources.
- **Currency** below means both the unit of money, where relevant, and the observation’s date/freshness. “Publication date not stated” is intentional; a page’s access date is not its publication date.
- **Confidence** rates support for the narrow claim as worded, not the overall opportunity. High confidence in a published corridor figure does not imply high confidence in an addressable market.
- The internal documents are product decisions, hypotheses, and evidence summaries—not independent customer validation. Public pages were accessed/reviewed on 18–23 November 2026 unless otherwise stated. Dynamic pages and vendor claims may change.

## Source-supported conclusions

- UK→ZW is a credible corridor to investigate because official totals are material and persistent across the 2024 and H1 2025 tables. Public evidence does **not** establish fee-specific TAM or Senda’s likely capture.
- Education is a plausible remittance purpose, and published Zimbabwe school/payment software shows term billing and several established collection methods. Evidence does **not** establish that the specific pilot schools use the proposed templates, will maintain a second ledger, or will accept open pay codes.
- Senda’s proposed neutral allowlist, student association, amount-due check, unique reference, and shared record form a coherent product hypothesis. Competing purpose-pay offers mean the gap is not “no one pays schools”; the differentiator must be partner-neutral connectivity/reconciliation that buyers will pay for.
- A relational Postgres model is a reasonable prototype fit for fee obligations, attempts, references, confirmations, and proofs. This is a technical fit judgment, not evidence that Next.js is uniquely best or that the architecture is production-ready.
- Bank USD and school-owned EcoCash are plausible candidate payout destinations based on school-side examples and public consumer payout methods. The exact business-account/merchant payout path and reference propagation are not verified for a named MTO.
- Bharat Connect is useful as a process comparison for fetch-then-pay and reference binding. It is a different regulated clearing and settlement system; Senda must not imply a guarantee or independent settlement proof it does not provide.

## Business-model audit

### Problem fit: MTOs

The MTO proposition is strongest if a partner wants to launch school-fee payments but does not want to onboard every school, maintain student/term dues, or build exception handling. The sources establish that purpose-pay competitors articulate similar needs, not that an MTO has this need or will outsource it to Senda. Public remittance payout pages also do not demonstrate the business-recipient capability Senda needs.

Key objections remain open: minimum useful school coverage; partner’s ability to pay a school account or merchant wallet; custom reference and reconciliation payload; partner liability for incorrect dues; school onboarding/KYB ownership; integration effort and procurement; and whether a buyer wants Senda to settle. If the partner requires Senda to collect/FX/payout, the product scope, compliance, and economics change materially.

### Problem fit: billers/schools

Schools have a credible need for correct attribution, reduced “who paid?” support, and a usable record for bursar operations. The pack’s source material supports varied payment channels and software handling of fee schedules, invoices, and receipts. It does not prove that Senda’s added process reduces costs versus the school’s existing student reference, bank statements, Paynow, EcoCash, or an incumbent purpose-pay provider.

The proposed product may add rather than remove work: a school must verify its legal entity and destination, keep fee obligations current, record external/local payments or accept stale flags, and handle disputes. A second ledger without integration can be valuable only if the resulting payer support and reconciliation savings exceed this burden. Interview bursars with actual statements and exception cases; quantify time spent and current failure rates before claiming savings.

### Strongest initial market wedge

**Retain UK→ZW private-school fees as the narrowest strategic test**, because it combines a material corridor, recurring term bills, a person-to-organisation attribution problem, and multiple school-side collection methods. Schools better exercise the Link + Obligation + proof concept than utilities, where a meter/account identifier can dominate and incumbent bill-pay is mature. This is a product-fit judgment, not a statistically proven market ranking: the wedge scorecard is internally assigned, and schools tie utilities numerically.

Do not expand the claim to all Zimbabwe schools, the whole UK diaspora, or a measured school-fee TAM. The counterfactuals are real: an integrated wallet can bypass Senda; Topa/ClicknPay-like products may provide purpose pay end to end; traditional MTOs may build internally; utilities may offer faster, lower-onboarding volume. If school settlement evidence or buyer appetite fails, reconsider the wedge instead of relying on the lock.

### Architecture fit and delivery reality

**Fit:** Next.js + TypeScript + Postgres/Prisma supports the current API/UI prototype and relational consistency for references and attempts. Decimal amounts and unique keys exist in the schema. Manual, seeded onboarding is appropriate for a sandbox.

**Not yet delivered / not yet proven:** the stack lock specifies S3-compatible proof storage, but `proofs.ts` writes local files; the seed includes a plain-text password stub and Biller access is a prototype; fee templates/obligations are seeded rather than a demonstrated import/bulk-generation workflow; there is no ERP, school-ledger, bank, wallet, or live MTO integration. There is no live balance freshness or school-side credit confirmation. API and code names should not be confused with production readiness. No production hosting, data residency, availability, disaster recovery, audit, access-control, privacy, or operating-cost evidence appears in this business pack.

The 95-case test pack is a valuable specification. Its successful validator run checks the pack and documentation, not all 95 runtime behaviors. Before pilot claims, convert the must-have paths into automated API tests and verify at least one real partner’s capability matrix and one school’s actual reconciliation artifacts.

### Pricing and unit economics

Pricing is explicitly deferred, and that restraint is appropriate. The research provides no Senda buyer, buyer budget, willingness-to-pay, competitor quote comparison, transaction-cost data, conversion, retention, support cost, or gross margin. Advertised school-software prices are not evidence of Senda pricing or willingness to pay for an additional service. A 5.04% corridor remittance cost is not Senda’s addressable revenue pool.

Treat models as experiments, not decisions: (a) partner-paid per-successful/confirmed payment; (b) partner platform or corridor fee; (c) school-paid subscription for reconciliation/proof; or (d) a hybrid with a capped onboarding/operations charge. For each, test who receives measurable value, who signs, integration/support cost, failure/dispute liability, pass-through to sender, and sensitivity to low school/transaction density. Avoid claiming a fee is justified by prevented diversion before a local baseline exists. Instrument validation, instruction, confirm, exception, proof access, and human operations from the pilot, without charging until terms are tested.

### Regulatory and operating assumptions

The product doctrine assigns collection, FX, and payout to a partner and says Senda does not hold funds. The reviewed research includes India BBPS rules and some Zimbabwe insurer-supervision material, but not a legal opinion covering Senda’s actual UK and Zimbabwe services. A technology/instruction/proof layer is not automatically outside payment-services, remittance, agency, AML/CFT, consumer-protection, privacy, or data-transfer requirements. The relevant perimeter depends on contractual role and actual funds/control flows.

Before any live payment, obtain written UK and Zimbabwe counsel/regulatory analysis covering: payment initiation/arranging or agency characterization; partner and institution responsibilities; AML/sanctions/KYB allocation; third-party school-beneficiary payouts; handling children’s/student data; UK GDPR and Zimbabwe data-protection/cross-border transfers; receipts, complaints, refunds, and false confirmation; and record-retention/security obligations. Map actual flow-of-funds and control in a diagram and contracts. Do not rely on “partner moves money” or #10’s lock as legal conclusions.

### Pilot assumptions and evidence threshold

The M6 “green” loop is a **sandbox product demonstration**, not a live payment pilot: a seeded institution, demo key, and partner stub can emit an attestation; a matching confirmation produces proof. It can establish that a workflow/API works, not customer need, partner willingness, money movement, school receipt, reduced diversion, or revenue.

Suggested staged gates before calling anything a commercial pilot:

1. **Discovery:** documented interviews with at least one MTO product/operations/compliance owner, multiple bursars, and parents who actually remit school fees; record current paths, failure cases, service-level expectations, and price reactions. Do not pre-commit to a fixed sample as statistical validation.
2. **Partner feasibility:** written confirmation of school/business-account and school-owned EcoCash payout eligibility, amount/limit/speed, supported reference fields, settlement status semantics, dispute path, fees, and compliance responsibilities.
3. **School feasibility:** named institutions consent to KYB and data use; verify destination ownership; test fee-template import against a real term register and local-payment exceptions; define who maintains and signs off each due.
4. **Trust/evidence design:** distinguish partner “sent/paid” from recipient “credited”; agree acceptable evidence, pending window, false-positive correction, and school dispute handling. Do not mark an MTO attestation as independent receipt proof in external claims.
5. **Commercial test:** present actual pricing alternatives to decision-makers and measure signed intent/paid trial, not hypothetical enthusiasm; record onboarding and support time and contribution economics.
6. **Live outcome:** only after legal/partner approval, reconcile real transactions to source artifacts and report denominators, missing confirmations, exceptions, time-to-credit, school acknowledgement, and refunds/disputes. Separate live results from test fixtures.

## Recommendations, ordered

1. Keep the wedge as a **time-boxed hypothesis**; remove “real and large” as a claim about school-fee demand and avoid TAM multiplication from remittance totals or survey percentages.
2. Remove the 15–20% diversion “working number” and 35–75% informal-flow range from pitches pending a Zimbabwe-specific, denominator-clear source.
3. Prioritize MTO and bursar discovery over further feature expansion. The key validation questions are “will you buy/use this?” and “can you actually pay this verified business destination with a useful reference?”
4. Resolve trust semantics: current `proven` is matched attestation. Use copy like “partner confirmation matched” until independent credit evidence or school acknowledgment is available. Model unpaid disputes, pending windows, and correction/reversal.
5. Treat pricing, school coverage, KYB conversion/time, fee freshness, and settlement SLAs as pilot measures—not locked assumptions. Define explicit stop/revisit thresholds with founders before spending on production integrations.
6. Complete regulatory and data-protection advice before live flow. Keep Senda out of custody/FX/payout unless a separately approved model justifies the scope.
7. Preserve the relational MVP architecture, but stage production claims: implement real auth/RBAC, S3-compatible durable storage, auditability, executable API tests, data retention and operational controls only as actual pilots require. Do not use the stack score of 4.7/“0.85 confidence” as independent validation.

## Missing evidence (decision-critical)

- MTO interviews, letters of intent, integration specification, sandbox access, institutional-pay eligibility, custom-reference behavior, operational payout terms, and agreed proof/status semantics.
- Named school/bursar pilot commitments; actual fee ledgers/registers; accepted destination account ownership; term-refresh willingness; current reconciliation/support cost; KYB completion time and refusal rate.
- Diaspora parent research in this exact corridor, including current payment paths, school-specific volumes/frequency, diversion or non-proof rates, price sensitivity, and alternatives used.
- Current, comparable competitor school coverage, active users/transactions, buyer type, settlement model, pricing, and product terms (not marketing alone).
- Zimbabwe-specific school-fee remittance data; an updated and scoped diaspora population estimate; recurring/seasonal volumes; feasible number of initial institutions. No defensible TAM/SAM/SOM in the reviewed material.
- A sourced 2026 corridor pricing benchmark and actual Senda willingness-to-pay/conversion evidence.
- Written legal/regulatory and privacy analysis for both operating jurisdictions, including student-data use and cross-border transfer.
- Live evidence of school receipt (bank/wallet feed, acceptable independent artifact, or school confirmation); time-to-credit and failure/dispute outcomes.
- Verified production architecture, hosting/data residency, security/access-control, uptime, retention, support and unit-cost evidence.
